# Access, equity and adult learning

Learning continues outside schools: at work, at home, in community programs and throughout adulthood. A tool’s potential value depends on affordability, connectivity, language, accessibility and the time and support available to use it. A learner should have meaningful alternatives when a particular tool is inaccessible or inappropriate. These are Dux design commitments, not measured impacts of a particular product. [S02](07-source-records.md#s02) [S03](07-source-records.md#s03)

## Access is more than availability

Providing an account does not ensure a tool works with assistive technology, provides suitable explanations, respects privacy or supports a learner’s language. Evaluate real workflows with the people who use them. Avoid treating no-AI practice as a rigid requirement when a tool is part of an accessibility accommodation. Review both benefits and risks of the actual configuration.

## Adult learners and transfer

The Harvard physics experiment supplies evidence from an undergraduate course, not all adult education. Its participants and course content may differ from workplace training, community education or independent study. The study measured immediate post-tests; it cannot establish durable transfer to other tasks. [S08](07-source-records.md#s08)

The research base collected here leaves significant gaps in older adult learners, adult basic education, disability, multilingual contexts, and low-connectivity settings. Future additions should seek primary studies with explicit comparators and document missing or conflicting findings. [S07](07-source-records.md#s07) [S09](07-source-records.md#s09)

# Evidence for adults and lifelong learning

Adult learning includes formal university study, professional training, family learning and self-directed curiosity. These are different settings with different constraints. The current Dux corpus contains several adult examples but does not represent all adult learners, occupations, languages or accessibility needs.

## Higher education

The Harvard physics study compared a particular tutor at home with active learning in class across two topics and reported higher immediate post-test scores for the tutor condition. The complete-case requirements, one course and changed mode and environment limit generalization. [S08](07-source-records.md#s08)

The online MBA preprint studied one finance course and a course-grounded tutor against access to ordinary resources, including consumer AI. Its researcher assessment had substantial missingness; a separate instructor final covered all randomized students. It does not prove that voice or a particular interface generally produces deeper learning. [S21](07-source-records.md#s21)

## Professional learning

In Peru, a randomized package for sixth-grade teachers combined training, a licence and follow-up. The authors measured teacher AI-related skills and reported no time savings on seven core tasks. They did not establish an improvement in pupils’ learning. Professional learning and student learning should not be conflated. [S16](07-source-records.md#s16)

A medical-school deployment studied usage and feedback, not a randomized learning outcome. Some students used the tool for clarification, but the researchers did not systematically assess output accuracy and could not reconstruct individual use patterns from the conversation-level data. [S10](07-source-records.md#s10)

## Missing perspectives

There is limited primary evidence in this collection about older adult learners, foundational literacy programs, apprenticeships, disabled adult learners and communities with limited connectivity. Involving these learners in design is a proposed practice; research on outcomes and unintended effects is still needed. [S14](07-source-records.md#s14) [S15](07-source-records.md#s15)

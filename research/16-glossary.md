# A glossary for the library

## Artificial intelligence

A broad term for systems designed to perform tasks associated with prediction, classification, generation or decision support. Different systems use different techniques; a generative chatbot is one type, not the whole field.

## Generative AI and language models

Generative systems produce content such as text or images. A language model generates text from patterns learned during training. A fluent response is not itself evidence of factual accuracy, understanding or a reliable source. The design and capabilities of systems change over time.

## AI literacy

The knowledge, skills and values needed to understand, question and engage with AI. UNESCO’s student framework includes human agency, ethics, techniques and design; it does not reduce literacy to prompting. [S02](07-source-records.md#s02)

## Scaffolding and feedback

Support that helps a learner work toward a goal, such as a question, hint, example or feedback. The term does not guarantee a benefit: the content and timing of support need evaluation. The two tutors in the Turkish study used different prompts and teacher-provided materials. [S01](07-source-records.md#s01)

## Retrieval-augmented generation

A design that supplies retrieved material to a generative model when composing an answer. Relevant source material may make answers easier to inspect, but retrieval does not guarantee correctness. The medical-school deployment did not systematically assess its response accuracy. [S10](07-source-records.md#s10)

## Outcome, comparator and unit

An outcome is what a study measures, such as a later test score. A comparator is the alternative against which the intervention is assessed. The unit might be a person, classroom, student-session, conversation or model output. These must not be interchanged. [S01](07-source-records.md#s01) [S09](07-source-records.md#s09)

## Association and causal effect

An association is a relationship observed in data; it need not mean one variable caused the other. Randomized comparisons can support causal conclusions about the assigned intervention under their design and assumptions, but missing data, bundled interventions and limits to generalization remain important.

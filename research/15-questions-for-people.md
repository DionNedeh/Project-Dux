# Questions for learners, families and educators

There is no single right sequence for every learner. These prompts support conversation; they are not an assessment, a validated safety instrument, or a substitute for an educator’s or caregiver’s judgment.

## For a learner

What do I hope to understand? Can I make a first attempt, alone or with the support I need? Would a hint or a person help more than an answer? What evidence would make me change my mind? Could I explain my conclusion? When would I choose to pause?

## For a family

What is age-appropriate for this child and this service? Can we try an activity together? Does the child know they can stop and speak to a trusted adult? What information does the service collect and how can we report a problem? UNICEF’s guidance centres children’s rights, safety and development, rather than offering a universal safe age. [S05](07-source-records.md#s05)

## For an educator

What learning objective is served? Which students may be excluded? What will remain human-led? How will feedback and generated facts be checked? Can students demonstrate their learning through an accessible alternative? How will the approach be evaluated and adjusted? UNESCO’s teacher framework treats agency and professional learning as core concerns. [S03](07-source-records.md#s03)

## For an institution

Who approves the service, reviews the agreement, manages data and responds when something goes wrong? What other approach could meet the same need? What evidence would cause a pause? Are there adequate time and resources for educators to make the process meaningful? [S11](07-source-records.md#s11) [S13](07-source-records.md#s13)

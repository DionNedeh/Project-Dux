# Study design: reading claims carefully

Evidence can be useful without answering every question. A careful reader can separate what a study observed, what its design supports, and what remains a proposal.

## Who and what was randomized?

A trial may assign people, tutors, classrooms or schools. Participants in a cluster share conditions, so the number of learners is not automatically the number of independent randomized units. Tutor CoPilot assigned tutors; the Peru study assigned schools after network-level selection. The design and analysis must account for this structure. [S19](07-source-records.md#s19) [S16](07-source-records.md#s16)

## How many outcomes were actually observed?

Enrollment, randomization and outcome completion can differ. The computer-science preprint randomized 979 course enrollees but analyzed 431 complete cases across all phases. A reduced sample may differ from those missing; it does not become 979 independent observations because that many were initially assigned. [S20](07-source-records.md#s20)

## What was the alternative?

A program may bundle extra time, trained adults and technology. The Nigeria study compares its combined after-school package with ordinary instruction; it cannot isolate the effect of the model from those other ingredients. A general tool, a teacher-informed tutor and a human tutor may all be meaningful comparators for different questions. [S09](07-source-records.md#s09)

## What did the test actually measure?

An exit ticket, an exam, a prompt-quality rubric, a self-report and a conversation count are not interchangeable. A short-term assessment can reveal an immediate difference without proving lasting retention or transfer. A finding that is not statistically significant is not proof that two approaches are equal. A source’s policy recommendation is not a measured intervention effect. [S01](07-source-records.md#s01) [S19](07-source-records.md#s19)

## What was accessible?

An abstract may omit recruitment, missing data or adverse outcomes; a dataset card is not necessarily the paper. Access limitations in the [source records](07-source-records.md) describe the material Dux actually inspected. An inaccessible supplement is an unknown, not evidence of an absence.

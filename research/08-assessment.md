# Assessment in an AI-rich setting

Assessment asks what a learner understands, can do and can explain. The answer may differ when a tool is available from when it is not. The Turkish mathematics study illustrates that distinction on immediate post-practice exams, but does not specify a universal assessment policy. [S01](07-source-records.md#s01)

## Choose the construct first

A learner may need to demonstrate independent arithmetic, evaluate a source, revise a draft, collaborate or use an accessibility tool. These are different constructs. An assessment that bans every aid may exclude a learner without measuring the intended ability; a task that permits automatic solutions may not show whether a foundational skill was acquired. Teachers should decide what they want to learn about the learner, and design an accessible assessment accordingly. This is an educational design proposal, not a trial result.

## Evidence in context

The undergraduate physics study used pre- and immediate post-tests after two short lessons. The Nigerian study used a paper assessment after a six-week program and had unequal follow-up completion. Neither should be read as proof of long-term transfer. [S08](07-source-records.md#s08) [S09](07-source-records.md#s09)

## Possible approaches to evaluate

Use an explanation or short conversation alongside a finished product; compare an initial attempt with a revision; ask a learner to check a deliberately flawed answer; or offer an appropriately accessible independent task. Assess the approach for fairness, workload and whether it measures the stated goal. Do not infer misconduct solely from a detector or an AI-like writing style; the sources here do not establish a reliable detection method.

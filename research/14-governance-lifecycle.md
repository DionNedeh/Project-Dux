# Governance across the life of a tool

A decision to introduce AI in education does not end at procurement. Institutions can define who is accountable, how risks and benefits will be assessed, when a tool will be reviewed, and how people can contest a harmful outcome. NIST’s voluntary, general-purpose AI framework organizes its work around govern, map, measure and manage. It is not a school-specific validation or a checklist. [S13](07-source-records.md#s13)

## Govern and map

Identify the decision owner, affected learners and educators, purpose, plausible harms and benefits, alternatives, data flows and limits of the proposed use. Include people who will use or be affected by the service. Map power as well as technology: who can decline, who can contest an outcome, and who carries the work of checking?

## Measure and respond

Define what counts as success and harm, the comparison, acceptable uncertainty and a route for incident reporting. Check actual performance in context, including accessibility and output quality. Set conditions for pausing, changing or retiring the tool. Review when the model, provider, terms or population changes. These are Dux proposals inspired by the framework, not proof of compliance with it.

## Protect agency

Human review requires someone with the authority, time and information to disagree with the tool. A nominal approval step without an ability to change the decision may not provide meaningful accountability. UNESCO’s teacher framework places human agency and responsibility at the centre of educators’ competencies. [S03](07-source-records.md#s03)

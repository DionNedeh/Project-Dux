# Teacher work, support and agency

Teachers decide what to teach, notice misunderstanding, build relationships, adapt materials and assess learning. Tools may assist with particular tasks, but an output is not self-validating and time saved on one activity may be spent elsewhere. UNESCO’s teacher competency framework emphasises human agency, responsible use, pedagogy and continuing professional learning. [S03](07-source-records.md#s03)

## Time, quality and workload

A World Bank data-catalogue description of a randomized program in Peru reports increased adoption but no measured time savings on core teacher tasks. The program included training, a licence and follow-up; its full paper and outcome methods have not yet been examined here. Treat this as a research lead, not a settled claim about all teachers or tools. [S16](07-source-records.md#s16)

Ask whether a proposed use genuinely reduces workload after reviewing, correcting, documenting and explaining outputs. Measure quality and equity alongside speed. Consult teachers about what work they want supported and what decisions should remain theirs. These are proposed evaluation questions, not established effects.

## Preparing and reviewing materials

A teacher can use a system to generate alternatives or examples, then check facts, appropriateness, accessibility and representation against trusted materials. They can also choose a non-AI process. A human review step needs time and authority to reject or revise an output. Keep personal student data out of a tool unless the specific use has been assessed and authorized. [S03](07-source-records.md#s03) [S11](07-source-records.md#s11)

## Professional learning

Consider practical evaluation, privacy, bias, human accountability and pedagogical design, not only prompting. A framework can specify valuable competencies without proving a particular workshop achieves them. Evaluate training over time with teachers and learners, and give people an accessible path to raise concerns.

# How AI is being used: what surveys show

Surveys tell us how many people say they use AI for learning and teaching, and what they think of it. They cannot tell us whether that use helps. The figures below come from different organisations, ages, questions and definitions, so they should be read side by side, not stitched into one trend.

## Teenagers in the United States

In autumn 2024, 26% of US teens told Pew they had ever used ChatGPT for schoolwork, double the 2023 share; older teens and Black and Hispanic teens reported more use. [S31](07-source-records.md#s31) A year later, with a broader question about any AI chatbot, 54% said they had used one for schoolwork help, and 10% said they did all or most of their schoolwork with chatbot help. [S32](07-source-records.md#s32) The two questions differ, so the rise from 26% to 54% is not a measured trend.

## Younger children

A spring 2026 Common Sense Media survey of 1,204 children aged 9 to 17 used a broad definition of AI that included search-result summaries. Among the children who used AI, 85% said they used it for schoolwork, and 16% of those who used it for homework said they had difficulty starting or finishing homework without it. Nearly three in four said their school or a teacher had talked about what AI use was allowed. [S34](07-source-records.md#s34) All of these are children’s own reports.

## Teachers

In a spring 2025 Gallup and Walton Family Foundation survey of 2,232 US public school teachers, six in ten reported using AI for work. Weekly users estimated saving 5.9 hours a week, a figure the report extrapolates to “six weeks a year”. Only 19% said their school had an AI policy, and 68% had received no AI training. [S33](07-source-records.md#s33) The time figure is teachers’ own estimate. In a randomized programme for teachers in Peru, measured time savings across seven tasks were not significant. [S16](07-source-records.md#s16) Time savings are contested and measured in different ways.

## Reading survey figures

- Check who was asked, when, and exactly what the question said.
- “Ever used” is not “uses often”; “AI” may include search summaries; a named product is not every chatbot.
- Surveys that share a panel are not independent confirmation of each other.
- Self-reports of time saved, cheating by others or difficulty without AI are perceptions, not measurements.

These surveys are all from the United States. Use elsewhere, and its effects on learning, are not established by them. See [reading claims carefully](22-reading-studies.md) and [access and actual use](23-access-and-use.md).

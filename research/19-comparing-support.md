# Four studies of educational support

A study can test access to a tool, a tutor’s response to suggestions, a training package or a complete learning program. These interventions cannot be treated as interchangeable. Each result depends on the comparison, participants, outcome and time frame.

## A tutor supporting a student

In the Turkish mathematics experiment, the standard GPT-4 interface increased assisted practice performance but was followed by lower unassisted exam performance than notes and textbooks. The teacher-informed tutor avoided much of that loss but did not show an exam advantage over control. It was one school and a short-term assessment. [S01](07-source-records.md#s01)

## A tool supporting a human tutor

In the US Tutor CoPilot preprint, tutors rather than children were randomized to access suggestions in live mathematics tutoring. The reported exit-ticket pass rate rose from 62% to 66%. Tutor attrition, age-inappropriate suggestions, provider involvement and the short-term outcome matter. Sessions and chat messages are not students. This does not test replacing human tutors. [S19](07-source-records.md#s19)

## A program for teachers

The Peru trial randomized school-level access to a package of training, a licence and follow-up. Its working paper reports improved performance on certain AI-use assessments without measured time savings across seven self-reported tasks. Student achievement was not measured as a demonstrated outcome. The package’s components cannot be separated by the main comparison. [S16](07-source-records.md#s16)

## A structured tutor for adults

A small online MBA preprint compared access to a course-grounded tutor with a holdout where consumer AI remained available. Only 51 of 86 randomized students completed both researcher assessments; a separate instructor final was available for all 86. It reports a positive direction, but its setting and assessment choices prevent sweeping claims about adult learning. [S21](07-source-records.md#s21)

## What can be compared

For each, ask: who was randomized, what changed together, what was measured, how many people completed the outcome, and how long did the effect last? A product demonstration, satisfaction survey, performance with assistance and unassisted retention each answer different questions. [Method](06-method-and-questions.md)

# Research method and open questions

## Scope and selection

Snapshot: 12 November 2026. This is a targeted collection of primary research and official guidance about AI literacy, education, independent judgment and child safeguards. Search focused on UNESCO, UNICEF, journals and study authors. It is not a systematic review: there is no exhaustive search strategy, formal screening flow or risk-of-bias meta-analysis. Relevant contradictory evidence may be missing.

Source notes identify the source version, design, population or non-study status, accessible material, claims and limitations. We link to primary materials instead of copying third-party articles. Publisher access failure is not evidence that a document or a result does not exist. Policy recommendations, product claims, experimental observations and Dux design suggestions have different evidentiary status.

## Questions for the next research round

1. What teaching approaches improve calibrated judgment over time, and for whom?
2. When do scaffolds improve later transfer rather than performance only while a tool is available?
3. How do results compare with equal-time teacher-led or peer learning, and what do learners value?
4. How do accessibility tools change the meaning of independent work and assessment?
5. What safeguards can be evaluated with children and caregivers without exposing them to avoidable harm?
6. How do outcomes vary by language, prior knowledge, connectivity, disability, age and socioeconomic context?
7. How should institutions detect and correct inaccuracies, privacy harms or inequitable outcomes after deployment?

## Maintenance

Recheck volatile policies and product terms at use. Record source version, date, access and correction history before changing claims. Preserve material conflicts. Confirm source links and exact denominators, review a representative range of evidence including adverse results, and assess accessibility before publishing. [Source records](07-source-records.md)

## Second review round

On 12 November 2026, twelve sources were added (S23 to S34) on kinds of help, effort and reliance, tutoring in lower-income settings, AI-text detection, assessment design and survey evidence on use. Two candidates were not added because their official texts could not be inspected: the education provisions of the EU AI Act, which commentary reports were amended in 2026, and two RAND reports on AI use in US schools. They remain open leads. The round did not find long-term studies of everyday AI use, or studies of young children using general-purpose chatbots on their own; both remain gaps.

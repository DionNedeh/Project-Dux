# Comparing three studies

The studies below are useful because they do not deliver one simple answer. Each evaluates a different package of support, population, comparison and outcome. None should be used as a ranking of people or a prediction for a particular learner.

## High-school mathematics in Türkiye

A classroom-assigned study compared textbook-and-notes practice with two GPT-4 designs, then used unassisted exams within each session. The general interface improved practice but reduced the subsequent exam score relative to control; the teacher-informed tutor largely mitigated the loss. One school and short-term outcomes constrain generalization. [S01](07-source-records.md#s01)

## Undergraduate physics in the United States

A crossover study compared a purpose-built AI tutor used at home with active learning in class. Immediate post-test outcomes favored the tutor under the study conditions. The comparison changes mode and setting together, and does not establish durable effects. [S08](07-source-records.md#s08)

## After-school learning in Nigeria

A randomized, six-week program offered structured content, teacher support and AI to interested students, compared with regular instruction. The program improved measured scores in the reported analysis; endline attrition differed by group. The design does not separate AI from extra time and other support. [S09](07-source-records.md#s09)

## What to ask next

Test similar tools against equal-time alternatives, measure delayed learning and transfer, include accessible and diverse settings, report missing data, and examine possible harms. Better task performance, user confidence, and learning are distinct outcomes. This is a research agenda, not a result already demonstrated.

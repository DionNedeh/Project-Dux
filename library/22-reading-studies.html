Study design: reading claims carefully

Reading evidence · 2 min read

On this page

  1. Who and what was randomized?
  2. How many outcomes were actually observed?
  3. What was the alternative?
  4. What did the test actually measure?
  5. What was accessible?

Evidence can be useful without answering every question. A careful reader can separate what a study observed, what its design supports, and what remains a proposal.

Who and what was randomized? #

A trial may assign people, tutors, classrooms or schools. Participants in a cluster share conditions, so the number of learners is not automatically the number of independent randomized units. Tutor CoPilot assigned tutors; the Peru study assigned schools after network-level selection. The design and analysis must account for this structure. S19 S16

How many outcomes were actually observed? #

Enrollment, randomization and outcome completion can differ. The computer-science preprint randomized 979 course enrollees but analyzed 431 complete cases across all phases. A reduced sample may differ from those missing; it does not become 979 independent observations because that many were initially assigned. S20

What was the alternative? #

A program may bundle extra time, trained adults and technology. The Nigeria study compares its combined after-school package with ordinary instruction; it cannot isolate the effect of the model from those other ingredients. A general tool, a teacher-informed tutor and a human tutor may all be meaningful comparators for different questions. S09

What did the test actually measure? #

An exit ticket, an exam, a prompt-quality rubric, a self-report and a conversation count are not interchangeable. A short-term assessment can reveal an immediate difference without proving lasting retention or transfer. A finding that is not statistically significant is not proof that two approaches are equal. A source’s policy recommendation is not a measured intervention effect. S01 S19

What was accessible? #

An abstract may omit recruitment, missing data or adverse outcomes; a dataset card is not necessarily the paper. Access limitations in the source records describe the material Dux actually inspected. An inaccessible supplement is an unknown, not evidence of an absence.

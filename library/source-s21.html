Structured tutoring and modality in an online MBA

Source: Shihao Yang, Marshall Van Alstyne and Chrysanthos Dellarocas, When AI Tutors Speak: Evidence from a Randomized Field Experiment, arXiv:2609.23958, working draft dated 20 September 2026. Preprint; peer review not established.

Access and design: Methods and selected results of the draft inspected. In one online MBA finance course, 86 consenting students were randomized to a structured course-grounded tutor or a holdout that could use consumer AI. Fifty-one completed both study assessment waves; the paper also reports instructor gradebook outcomes for all 86. Within the tutor arm, modality alternated weekly. The paper reports 32 students in the within-student voice/text comparison. Students, assessment waves, weekly interactions and course examinations are distinct units.

Finding and limits: The researchers report an advantage for tutor access on their assessment and a smaller same-direction difference on the instructor’s final. Attrition affects the custom-assessment analysis; the final was not itself a preregistered outcome. No general conclusion about all adults or all voice tutors follows, and a non-significant modality difference does not by itself prove equivalence. The tutor, subject, cohort and short field period bound the findings.

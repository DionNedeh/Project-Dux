Learning and independence

Independent learning · 2 min read

On this page

  1. A thoughtful pause
  2. When to step away
  3. Check the outcome, not just the impression

A finished task and a learned skill are different outcomes. In the Turkish mathematics study, standard AI assistance improved practice performance but was followed by lower performance on the same session’s unassisted exam. A specially constructed tutor with teacher-provided solutions and hints had a different result. This is a warning about a specific design and outcome, not proof that AI inevitably weakens thinking. S01

A thoughtful pause #

Before using a tool, ask: What am I trying to learn? What can I attempt first? What help would move my thinking forward? How will I check an answer? What would I be able to explain to another person? A learner may choose a hint over a complete solution, work with a peer, or return to a book. These are suggestions rather than rules, and reasonable accommodations can change what independence looks like.

When to step away #

Consider a pause when the activity calls for independent practice, when answers cannot be checked, when the interaction becomes frustrating or distracting, or when private information would otherwise be shared. For sensitive personal concerns, speak with an appropriate trusted person. There is no evidence here for a universal time limit or an obligation to avoid AI altogether.

Check the outcome, not just the impression #

The Turkish researchers observed a mismatch between students’ perceptions and exam outcomes. An independent explanation, later application or appropriately accessible assessment can offer more information than confidence alone. These checks should be proportionate, support learners rather than punish them, and be compared with alternatives. Long-term effects remain open. S01

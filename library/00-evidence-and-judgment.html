Evidence and judgment

Reading evidence · 3 min read

On this page

  1. What the evidence shows
  2. How to read the differences
  3. Where to start

Dux asks how people can learn about AI, learn with it, and retain the freedom and capacity to learn without it. An AI-generated answer may be useful or wrong; a fluent answer alone tells us little about whether its reader has learned. The purpose of education can include curiosity, understanding, relationships, participation and independence, not merely a finished answer.

What the evidence shows #

In a Turkish high-school mathematics experiment, a GPT-4 interface improved assisted practice scores yet the same group scored lower on a subsequent unassisted exam than the control group. A teacher-informed tutor largely mitigated that deficit, without a detectable positive exam effect. The study concerns one school, four short sessions, and specific tools; it does not show all AI use impairs learning. S01

A Harvard undergraduate physics crossover experiment found better immediate post-test scores after a specifically designed AI tutor than after its active-learning class. Its sample, course, lessons, setting and design matter; the results cannot establish that AI generally outperforms teachers or that advantages persist. S08

A Nigerian pilot found higher assessment scores for an after-school program combining AI access, teacher support and structured content against ordinary schooling. Differential assessment completion and the absence of an equal-time human tutoring comparison limit attribution to AI alone. S09

How to read the differences #

These studies ask different questions. The Turkish study's subsequent exam was immediately after practice; the physics study compared two modes and topics; the Nigerian pilot compared an entire supplemental program with regular schooling. None establishes a universal ranking. A helpful research question is: which learners, doing what work, with what support, compared to what alternative, and measured when?

The UNESCO and UNICEF materials in this library are institutional guidance. Their recommendations are useful for designing learning and safeguards, but issuing a framework is not a test of its effectiveness. We label Dux suggestions as proposals, not validated interventions. S02 S03 S05

Where to start #

Explore AI literacy, teaching, independence, or children. The source records preserve access limitations and give direct links. The method and questions say what remains uncertain.

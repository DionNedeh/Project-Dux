Medical education observational study

Source: Thomas Thesen and Soo Hwan Park, “A generative AI teaching assistant for personalized learning in medical education,” npj Digital Medicine 8, 627, 4 November 2025. doi:10.1038/s41746-025-02022-1.

Access and design: Publisher methods, selected results and limitations inspected. Observational deployment of a course-material-grounded assistant across two medical-school cohorts, not a randomized learning-outcome trial. Among survey respondents, 22 of 56 in the first cohort and 23 of 87 in the second reported at least one use. The paper reports 328 conversations in thematic analysis (216 / 65.9% in its first topic category); conversations are not distinct participants, and topic codes can overlap. GPT-4o assisted thematic coding; a 15% subset received human validation. The authors did not systematically assess answer accuracy or track individual engagement patterns. Findings describe usage and reported experience in this setting; they do not establish learning gains or that retrieval eliminates hallucination.

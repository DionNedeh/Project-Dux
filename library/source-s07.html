A short literacy intervention

AI literacy · 2 min read

Source: Brett Puppart and Jaan Aru, A Brief AI Literacy Intervention Does Not Significantly Reduce Over-Reliance and Increases Under-Reliance on ChatGPT: A Randomized Study, arXiv:2503.10556v2, revised 18 September 2026; originally submitted 13 March 2025 under a different title. DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2503.10556.

Access and design: Selected methods, results and limitations of the 28-page v2 PDF inspected, as well as the version history. Preprint; peer-review or subsequent publication status not established. In October 2024 researchers invited 72 Estonian schools; 10 forwarded invitations. Forty-two real 12th-grade students completed the questionnaire; six were excluded for stated speed criteria, leaving 36 analyzed, ages 18–19: 19 intervention and 17 control. Individuals were randomized. Each saw eight selected math puzzles and fixed screenshots of model recommendations, four correct and four incorrect, and decided whether to adopt them; they did not interact live with the model. The control read a general introduction and the intervention received additional information. Participant and trial are distinct units.

Finding and limitations: Across participants, the reported mean proportion of incorrect recommendations adopted was 52.1%. The intervention group’s mean was 48.68% versus 55.88% for controls; the difference was not statistically significant (p = .471), which does not establish equivalence or no effect. Rejection of correct advice was higher in the intervention group (36.84% versus 19.12%; p = .025). The small sample, selection, exclusion criteria, fixed screenshots and short task constrain precision and generalization; the study does not establish that AI literacy education generally fails, or what happens in ordinary iterative chatbot use.

Research library / Source note S01 / 12 November 2026

Learning performance and unassisted assessment

Source: Hamsa Bastani, Osbert Bastani, Alp Sungu, Haosen Ge, Özge Kabakcı and Rei Mariman, “Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics,” PNAS 122(26), e2422633122, 25 June 2025, doi:10.1073/pnas.2422633122. The 20 August 2025 correction was inspected: it corrects Osbert Bastani’s affiliation after a production error; it reports no changes to findings.

Access and design: Publisher article and the relevant sections of the author-hosted manuscript and appendix inspected. The publisher-hosted supplement returned HTTP 403; equivalence to the author-hosted version was not independently established. The preregistration itself was not inspected. Preregistered classroom-assigned field experiment at one Turkish high school in fall 2023, grades 9–11. Four 90-minute sessions; approximately 50 classes and nearly 1,000 real students, not model-generated participants. The author-hosted appendix Table 3 reports 839 students in the main survey sample: 277 GPT Tutor, 320 control, and 242 GPT Base; its survey sample including honors contains 943 students. The author version says the main sample excludes honors classes and nonrespondents to the baseline survey. These counts should not be described as total enrollment. The publisher main Table 1 reports 2,848 student-session observations per outcome, not 2,848 people. Exact enrollment and class counts remain unverified. Unit of assignment: classroom; student-session performance analyzed, with classroom-clustered standard errors. Honors classes excluded from main sample. Students worked from teacher-provided problems, with notes/textbooks alone, a GPT-4 chat interface, or a teacher-informed GPT-4 tutor including solutions and hints, then took an unassisted exam.

Finding: Relative to controls, practice scores were higher with both AI variants; the standard interface group scored 17% lower on the subsequent unassisted exam relative to controls. The guarded tutor group did not show a positive exam effect relative to controls, and the negative effect was largely mitigated. These are short-term results for this setting and these specific tools; they do not establish long-term outcomes, effects on all ages, or that every guardrail works. Mechanism analyses suggest copying or reduced engagement, but do not definitively isolate the mechanism. See publisher methods and results.

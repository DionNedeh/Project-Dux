Teaching and implementation

The decision to use a tool starts with a learning goal and the people in the room. UNESCO’s teacher framework includes human agency, ethics, AI foundations, pedagogy and professional learning. It calls for teacher judgment and meaningful human interaction. It describes competencies; it does not validate a specific product or training course. S03

Design around the learning task

A teacher might invite an initial attempt, then offer a hint, comparison, feedback or an alternative explanation, and finally ask a learner to explain or apply the idea. A learner using assistive technology may need a different workflow. These are design hypotheses; the research does not prove that any single sequence is best. Compare learning outcomes, not just completion speed or satisfaction. S01 S08

Two positive studies, different comparisons

The Harvard study compared a purpose-built, content-grounded tutor used at home with in-class active learning over two topics. The paper reports immediate post-test advantages for that tutor. The conditions differ in environment and mode as well as tool; the result does not isolate a general effect of AI or assess durable learning. S08

The Nigerian program paired structured after-school sessions, teacher support and AI tools. Students who volunteered were randomized to the program or usual instruction. Results favor the program on the measured assessment, but substantial and unequal endline completion limits confidence, and no equal-time alternative isolates the tool. S09

Institutional practice

Before implementation, educators can document purpose, alternatives, accessibility, privacy and review arrangements; test materials for accuracy and bias; explain the intended role of the tool; and define how concerns are reported. Preserve human oversight for grading and consequential decisions. These are proposed practices informed by guidance, not a substitute for local law or a validated checklist. S03 S04

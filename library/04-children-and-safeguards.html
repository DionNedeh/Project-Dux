Children and safeguards

Children · 2 min read

On this page

  2. Conversational systems and relationships

Children are not a single age group. Development, disability, context, family circumstances and the system itself affect what is appropriate. UNESCO calls for age-appropriate educational design and privacy safeguards. UNICEF’s 2025 guidance sets out ten child-centred requirements including safety, data protection, fairness, accountability, development and inclusion. These are policy recommendations, not proof that a particular deployment is safe. S04 S05

Practical questions for adults and institutions #

Before use, check current product age requirements and applicable local rules; examine what personal information is requested, retained or shared; consider whether meaningful adult support and an accessible alternative are available; and establish a way to report harmful or misleading outputs. These are Dux proposals derived from rights-based guidance. They do not replace a product assessment or legal review.

Teach children that generated content can be mistaken, that they can stop an interaction, and that a trusted person can help. Avoid requiring sensitive personal disclosures to use an educational activity. In a classroom, educators should select and review activities appropriate to the learner and remain accountable for their use.

Conversational systems and relationships #

UNICEF’s 2026 policy brief identifies distinct child-rights concerns around chatbots and companion-like systems and discusses preventive oversight. Its landing page does not quantify how often harms occur. A conversational tool should not be presented as a substitute for trusted people or professional support. S06

What remains unknown #

This library does not establish a safe age, exposure duration, or proven universal safeguard. Evidence for younger children, long-term outcomes, disabled children and culturally diverse settings needs particular attention. Appropriate local safeguards may differ as systems and laws change.
